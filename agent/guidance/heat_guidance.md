# General heat-health guidance (paraphrased for grounding)

Source note: paraphrased from widely published public heat-health advice. It is not a
quotation of any official document; officers should verify against the current NDMA
guidelines and their city's Heat Action Plan before issuing orders.

## hydration
Drink water regularly even when not thirsty. Oral rehydration solution, lemon water and
buttermilk help. Avoid alcohol, tea, coffee and sugary drinks during hot spells.

## timing
Avoid heavy outdoor work and travel in the hottest part of the day, roughly 12 to 3 pm.
Shift outdoor work to early morning or evening where possible and give workers shade breaks.

## cooling spaces
Move people to a cool, shaded or ventilated place. Open community buildings, libraries and
clinics as cooling spaces during heatwaves. Provide drinking water at bus stops and markets.

## vulnerable groups
Older adults, young children, pregnant women, outdoor workers and people with chronic illness
are at higher risk. Check on them during heatwave days.

## heatstroke
Signs of heatstroke include confusion, very high body temperature, hot dry or very sweaty skin,
fainting or seizures. This is an emergency: move the person to a cool place, cool the body
with water, and call emergency services (112 or ambulance 108 in India).

## infrastructure
Shade structures, cool roofs, cool pavements and tree planting reduce local heat over time.
Cool roofs and shade work immediately; trees take years to reach full effect.
